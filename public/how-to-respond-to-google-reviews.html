How to respond to Google reviews

A practical guide for small business owners · Updated October 19, 2026

A Google review is a short public conversation. The customer speaks first, and your reply is what the next person reads before they call or book. Here is how to reply to Google reviews when you own the business and you post the answer yourself: name something real, sound like yourself, and stay calm when the stars are low.

Why a reply matters

People size up a business from the profile. An unanswered complaint leaves the last word with the unhappy customer. An unanswered compliment makes a good visit look ignored. A reply does not change the star rating. It shows someone is paying attention, and it shows the next customer how you act when a visit goes well or badly.

Your Google Business Profile is often the first page a local shopper opens. Hours, photos, and the phone number sit beside the reviews. Google’s help says that replying shows customers you value their feedback, and that helpful replies can help a business stand out. The reply is public, it appears as a response from the owner, and the reviewer is notified. You need a verified profile before you can post one. That is a trust signal, not a promise about rankings. Staff can also see the standard you want when a slow lunch hour or a similar problem shows up more than once.

How to reply to a positive review

Answer the praise, not only the complaints. A specific thank-you is often what makes the next visit feel easy. Keep it short:

  • Use their name when it looks real, and skip it when the profile is generic.
  • Mention one detail they wrote: the dish, the stylist, the on-time arrival, the quiet corner.
  • Invite them back in one sentence, then stop. Leave out discount codes and extra exclamation points.

Google’s tips for review replies also say to stay conversational instead of promotional, and to change at least one detail so the thank-you is not identical every time.

How to reply to a negative review

Write the reply as customer service other people will read, not as an argument to win. The author may never return. Quiet readers are deciding whether to visit.

Read it twice. Separate what you know from what you are guessing. Apologize for a cold meal if they say the food arrived cold. Do not confirm, in public, an accusation against an employee you have not checked.

  1. Thank them for writing.
  2. Apologize for the experience they described, without agreeing to facts you have not verified.
  3. Give one next step: call the shop, ask for the owner, or email an address you actually check.
  4. Stop after a couple of short paragraphs.

Take the details offline. A review thread is a poor place to rebuild a receipt or fight about a policy. Google’s guidance asks you to protect privacy, skip personal attacks, and move a complicated case to phone or email. If a review looks fake or breaks Google’s rules, flag it in your Business Profile, and still post a short calm reply while you wait. Removal is uncertain.

Where to post the reply

In your Business Profile, open the reviews and choose Reply. Google checks the text against its content policies, then shows it as a response from the business, not under your personal name.

Tone that sounds like a person

Write the way you talk at the counter. Use “I” if you are the owner, or “we” if the team is answering, and stick with it. A happy review can sound warm. A complaint should sound steady. One real detail beats three compliments. Read the draft aloud, and change anything you would not say in person. Skip “Dear valued customer,” adjective piles, hashtags, and a sign-off longer than the reply.

What not to do

  • Do not argue. Even when the facts are wrong, a line-by-line rebuttal makes the page louder. Later readers notice who stayed respectful.
  • Do not invent refunds. Skip the free meal, gift card, or discount you thought of while angry. Offer only what you will do when they contact you, and talk about money in private. Google also asks you not to pitch deals in the reply.
  • Do not ignore the review. Silence is loudest on a 1-star rating and on a profile that has sat still for months. A late reply still helps. Start with new reviews and serious ones.
  • Do not post private details. Leave out medical notes, full order histories, home addresses, and unverified accusations about staff.
  • Do not open with “please delete this.” Deal with the experience first. Never offer a perk for a changed or removed review. Google treats review incentives as a policy violation.

Example reply to a 5-star review

Sample review: “The oat latte was perfect and I loved the quiet corner by the window. I’ll be back after school drop-off.” Maya, 5 stars.

Thank you, Maya. Glad the oat latte and that window corner made the morning easier. We’ll be glad to see you the next time you’re in after drop-off.

It uses her name and two details she chose, and it invites her back without a coupon.

Example reply to a 1-star review

Sample review: “Waited 40 minutes and the pasta arrived cold. Nobody checked on us.” 1 star.

I’m sorry your meal took too long and arrived cold, and that no one checked on your table. That is not the visit we want anyone to have. Please call and ask for the manager, or email the address on our profile, and include the date you came in. I want to look up that table and talk with you directly.

It names the problems, skips a fight about the kitchen, and does not promise a free meal. For more cases, such as rude staff, price complaints, and fake reviews, see these negative review response examples.

A habit you can keep

Check reviews a few times a week, answer within a couple of days, name one real detail, and have someone else read an angry draft before you post it.

Questions owners ask

Should I reply to every Google review?

Reply to praise and to complaints. A few specific sentences are enough. If you are behind, start with new reviews and with anything serious.

How do I answer a 1-star Google review?

Thank them, apologize for what they described, and skip the argument. Ask them to call or email so the details stay private. Do not promise a refund you have not agreed to give.

Do replies help a Google Business Profile?

Google says a reply shows you value the feedback, and that helpful replies can help a business stand out. The reply does not replace a good visit. It shows the next customer that someone is watching the profile.
