Dentist review response examples

HIPAA-conscious replies for dental practices on Google, Yelp, and Healthgrades · Updated October 23, 2026

Dental practices have one extra rule most businesses don’t: you can’t confirm that a reviewer is a patient or mention any treatment details, even if they shared them first. Below are nine common dental reviews with replies that stay general, warm, and privacy-safe, plus a note on why each works. For the general method, read how to respond to Google reviews.

A quick formula for dental practice replies

  1. Thank the reviewer without confirming they are a patient (“Thank you for sharing your experience”).
  2. Speak to your policies and standards in general terms, never to their treatment, chart, or appointment.
  3. Apologize for how they felt and invite them to call the office manager directly.
  4. Sign with a name and role, like “Dr. Patel” or “Lisa, Office Manager.”

1. Long wait in the lobby

Sample review: “My appointment was at 9 and I wasn’t seen until almost 10. No one told us why.” 2 stars.

Thank you for letting us know. Waiting nearly an hour without an update is frustrating, and we’re sorry. Our front desk team should keep everyone informed when the schedule runs behind, and we’ve reviewed that with them. If you’d like to talk it through, please call our office manager, Lisa, at (555) 123-4567. – Dr. Patel

Why it works: It owns the communication gap and names a fix, without confirming anything about the reviewer’s visit.

2. Surprise bill after insurance

Sample review: “Was told my cleaning was covered and then got a $180 bill. Feels like a bait and switch.” 1 star.

We’re sorry for the confusion and frustration. Our goal is always to give clear cost estimates before any service, and insurance benefits can sometimes be processed differently than expected. Please call our billing coordinator at (555) 123-4567 so we can review this with you personally. – Lisa, Office Manager

Why it works: It explains the general process without discussing the reviewer’s account, and moves the money conversation offline.

3. Procedure was painful

Sample review: “The filling hurt way more than it should have and the dentist didn’t seem to care.” 2 stars.

Thank you for sharing this. Every patient deserves to feel heard and comfortable in our chair, and we’re sorry this experience fell short. We’d genuinely like to hear more. Please call Dr. Patel directly at (555) 123-4567. – Dr. Patel

Why it works: It doesn’t confirm that a filling was done or debate the pain, which would disclose care details. It offers a direct line to the doctor.

4. Rude front desk

Sample review: “The receptionist was short with me on the phone and hung up.” 2 stars.

We’re sorry to hear this. Everyone who calls our office should be treated with patience and respect. I’ve shared your feedback with our team, and I’d appreciate the chance to hear more. Please call me at (555) 123-4567. – Lisa, Office Manager

Why it works: It takes the complaint seriously without naming or blaming a staff member in public.

5. Pushy treatment recommendations

Sample review: “Went in for a cleaning and they tried to sell me $3,000 of work. Second opinion said I didn’t need it.” 2 stars.

Thank you for the feedback. We never want anyone to feel pressured. Our approach is to explain every option so patients can make their own decisions, and second opinions are always welcome. If you’d like to discuss your concerns, please call Dr. Patel at (555) 123-4567.

Why it works: It defends the practice’s philosophy calmly and supports second opinions, which builds trust with readers.

6. Anxious patient, happy result

Sample review: “I’ve avoided the dentist for 10 years. Everyone here was so patient and kind. I actually don’t dread going back.” 5 stars.

Thank you so much for these kind words! It means a lot to our team to hear that we helped make the visit comfortable. We look forward to seeing you at your next checkup. – Dr. Patel and team

Why it works: For positive reviews you can reflect what they wrote, but still avoid adding any details they didn’t share.

7. Kids’ dentist praise

Sample review: “Dr. Patel was amazing with my 5-year-old. She left with a smile and a sticker.” 5 stars.

This made our day, thank you! We love helping young smiles feel at home here. We’ll be sure to have plenty of stickers ready next time. – Dr. Patel

Why it works: It’s warm, short, and playful without mentioning any treatment.

8. Review from someone who isn’t a patient

Sample review: “Called to ask about prices and they wouldn’t give me a straight answer.” 1 star.

Thank you for reaching out to us. Because costs depend on each person’s needs and insurance, we can’t always quote exact prices over the phone, but we’re happy to provide a written estimate after a consultation. Please call (555) 123-4567 and ask for Lisa.

Why it works: It explains the reason in plain language and gives a clear next step.

9. Short 5-star review

Sample review: No text, 5 stars. 5 stars.

Thank you for the 5 stars! We appreciate you taking the time to leave a review.

Why it works: Short is fine. Notice it says nothing about a visit, which keeps it privacy-safe.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Confirming that the reviewer is a patient or mentioning their treatment, insurance, or appointment date. That can be a HIPAA violation even if they posted the details first.
  • Arguing about clinical decisions in public.
  • Offering refunds or free services in the reply.
  • Using the same canned “We value your feedback” line on every review.

Frequently asked questions

Can a dentist respond to Google reviews under HIPAA?

Yes, but replies must not confirm that the reviewer is a patient or reveal any treatment, billing, or appointment details. Keep responses general, speak about your policies, and invite the person to call the office.

Should a dental practice respond to negative reviews?

Yes. A calm, privacy-safe reply shows prospective patients that you take concerns seriously. Apologize for the experience, describe your general standard, and move the conversation to a phone call.

Can I mention the patient’s name in my reply?

It is safest not to. Addressing the reviewer by name can confirm they are a patient. A simple “Thank you for sharing your experience” works.

How fast should a dental office reply to reviews?

Aim for one to two business days. Assign one person, often the office manager, to check reviews daily.
