How to respond to patient reviews without violating HIPAA

For dentists, doctors, therapists, chiropractors, and other healthcare practices · Updated November 18, 2026

Replying to Google reviews is one of the easiest ways for a practice to build trust. But in healthcare, a well-meaning reply can become a privacy violation if it confirms that someone is a patient or mentions their care. Here’s how to reply to patient reviews, good and bad, without disclosing protected health information (PHI). For general reply basics, see how to respond to Google reviews.

This guide is general information, not legal advice. If you’re unsure about a specific reply, check with your privacy officer or healthcare attorney before posting.

The core rule: the patient can share, you can’t

A patient is free to post anything they want about their own visit. That doesn’t give your practice permission to add to it. Under HIPAA, even confirming that the reviewer is your patient can count as disclosing PHI. So the safest replies:

  • Don’t confirm or deny that the reviewer was ever seen at your practice.
  • Don’t mention dates, procedures, diagnoses, medications, test results, insurance, or billing details, even if the reviewer already did.
  • Don’t correct the reviewer’s version of events with facts from their chart.
  • Do speak about your practice’s policies and values in general terms, and invite them to contact you privately.

This has led to real fines

The HHS Office for Civil Rights settled with a California dental practice, New Vision Dental, over replies to Yelp reviews that disclosed patients’ information. The practice paid $23,000 and agreed to a corrective action plan. (HHS announcement.) The pattern in cases like this is the same: an owner gets upset at a negative review and responds with details from the patient’s record.

A safe structure for any patient review

  1. Thank them for the feedback, without saying “thanks for visiting” or “thanks for being our patient.”
  2. Speak in general terms about what your practice values or how you normally handle things.
  3. Move it offline with a phone number or email for your office manager.
  4. Keep it short. The more you write, the more chances to slip in a detail.

Example replies

Positive review that mentions a procedure

Review: “Dr. Patel did my root canal and I felt nothing. Best dentist ever!”

Thank you so much for the kind words! Our whole team works hard to make every visit as comfortable as possible, and it means a lot to hear this. – The Bright Smile Dental team

Why it works: It doesn’t repeat “root canal,” doesn’t confirm the person is a patient, and still sounds warm.

Negative review about a bill

Review: “They charged me $400 more than quoted and my insurance didn’t cover it.”

We’re sorry to hear about this frustration. We aim to be clear about costs before any treatment, and we take billing concerns seriously. Please call our office manager, Lisa, at (555) 123-4567 so we can look into it privately. – Bright Smile Dental

Why it works: No amounts, no insurance details, no confirmation of treatment. It shows readers a real path to resolution. More ideas in how to respond to reviews about price.

Negative review about wait times

Review: “Waited an hour past my appointment. Nobody apologized.”

We’re sorry for this experience. Long waits aren’t the standard we hold ourselves to, and we’re always working to keep our schedule on time and keep people updated when we fall behind. We’d appreciate the chance to talk with you directly at (555) 123-4567. – Desert Vista Family Medicine

Review that’s unfair or untrue

Review: “The doctor misdiagnosed me and refused to refill my prescription.”

Thank you for sharing your concerns. Privacy laws prevent us from discussing anyone’s care online, but we take every concern seriously and would welcome the chance to talk. Please contact our practice manager at (555) 123-4567. – Desert Vista Family Medicine

Why it works: It explains why you can’t respond in detail, which future readers understand, and doesn’t argue. See also how to respond without admitting fault.

Star-only review

Thank you for the 5 stars! We truly appreciate it.

Phrases to avoid

  • “We’re sorry your procedure was painful…”
  • “According to our records, you missed two appointments…”
  • “Thanks for coming in last Tuesday!”
  • “Your insurance denied the claim, not us.”
  • “We hope your recovery is going well.”

Each one confirms a patient relationship or reveals something about their care.

What if the review is fake or abusive?

If a review breaks Google’s policies (spam, harassment, someone who was never a customer), you can report it from your Business Profile. You still can’t say “this person was never our patient” in a public reply, since confirming or denying either way is risky. Keep the reply generic, or don’t reply while the report is pending. Common questions

Can I reply to a patient review at all under HIPAA?

Yes. Many practices reply to reviews. The key is to keep replies general: thank the reviewer, describe your practice’s policies or values, and invite them to contact you privately, without confirming they are a patient or mentioning any details of their care.

If the patient already shared their treatment in the review, can I mention it?

It’s safest not to. A patient sharing their own information doesn’t authorize your practice to disclose or confirm it. Reply as if you know nothing about the visit.

Can I thank a reviewer by name?

Using a name together with a reply that implies they’re a patient can be a disclosure. Many practices skip names on review replies and sign off with the practice name instead.

Should I reply to negative patient reviews?

Usually yes, with a short, calm, generic reply that offers a private way to follow up. Future patients mainly want to see that you take concerns seriously.
