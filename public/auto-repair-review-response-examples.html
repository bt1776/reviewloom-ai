Auto repair shop review response examples

For mechanics, tire shops, body shops, oil change, and dealer service departments · Updated October 23, 2026

Trust is everything for a repair shop: most drivers can’t judge the work themselves, so they judge how you treat people. Below are nine common auto repair reviews with a reply for each and why it works. For the general method, read how to respond to Google reviews.

A quick formula for auto repair shop replies

  1. Thank the customer and name the vehicle or service if they mentioned it.
  2. For complaints, apologize once and explain your general policy (approvals, warranties, diagnostics) in plain words.
  3. Offer a re-inspection or a call with the service manager rather than debating the repair online.
  4. Sign with a name and role, like “Ray, Service Manager.”

1. “They overcharged me”

Sample review: “Brought my car in for brakes and the bill was almost double what they quoted.” 1 star.

I’m sorry the bill was a surprise. Our policy is to call for approval before doing any work beyond the original quote. If that didn’t happen clearly, we want to make it right. Please call me at (555) 123-4567 so we can go over the invoice together. – Ray, Service Manager

Why it works: It states the approval policy and moves the details offline without arguing.

2. Same problem came back

Sample review: “Paid $600 to fix my check engine light and it came back on two days later.” 2 stars.

That’s frustrating, and we’re sorry. Our repairs carry a 12-month/12,000-mile warranty, and a light coming back that quickly is something we want to see right away. Please bring it in or call (555) 123-4567 and we’ll re-check it at no charge for diagnosis.

Why it works: It points to the warranty and gives a specific, fair next step.

3. Car took too long

Sample review: “Said it’d be ready Tuesday, didn’t get it back until Friday.” 2 stars.

Thank you for your patience, and we’re sorry for the delay. A part we ordered arrived late, but we should have updated you as soon as we knew. We now text customers whenever an estimated pickup time changes. – Ray

Why it works: One honest reason, plus the communication fix, beats a list of excuses.

4. Upselling complaint

Sample review: “Went in for an oil change and they gave me a list of $1,200 in ‘recommended’ services.” 3 stars.

Thanks for the feedback. We share what we see during every inspection so you can plan ahead, but nothing is done without your OK, and we always separate “needs it now” from “keep an eye on it.” Happy to walk through the list with you any time.

Why it works: It explains why recommendations happen and reassures readers there’s no pressure.

5. Damage claim

Sample review: “Got my car back with a scratch on the door that wasn’t there before.” 1 star.

We’re sorry to hear this and we take it seriously. Please call me directly at (555) 123-4567 so we can look at the car together and review our check-in photos. We want to resolve this fairly. – Ray, Service Manager

Why it works: It is calm, doesn’t admit or deny fault in public, and offers a fair process.

6. Rude service writer

Sample review: “The guy at the counter acted like I was stupid for asking questions.” 2 stars.

I’m sorry you were made to feel that way. Questions are always welcome here, and explaining repairs clearly is part of the job. I’ve shared this with our front counter team. – Ray, Service Manager

Why it works: It validates the customer and sets a clear standard.

7. Honest shop praise

Sample review: “They told me I didn’t need the $800 repair the dealer quoted. Just needed a $90 sensor. Customer for life.” 5 stars.

Thank you! We’d always rather earn your trust than an extra repair order. Glad we could get you back on the road for less, and we’ll see you at your next service.

Why it works: It reinforces the honesty theme readers care about most.

8. Quick tire or oil change

Sample review: “In and out in 25 minutes for new tires. Great price.” 5 stars.

Thanks for the 5 stars! Glad we got you in and out fast. Enjoy the new tires, and come see us for a free rotation in 5,000 miles.

Why it works: A short, useful reminder gives a reason to return without a public discount.

9. No text, 1 star

Sample review: No text, 1 star. 1 star.

We’re sorry your visit didn’t meet expectations. We’d like to know what happened so we can fix it. Please call us at (555) 123-4567 and ask for Ray.

Why it works: It’s short and professional, and shows readers you respond to every concern.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Explaining technical details in a way that makes the customer look ignorant.
  • Posting the customer’s invoice, VIN, or plate number.
  • Arguing about whether a part was “really” bad.
  • Promising free work publicly before you have inspected the car.

Frequently asked questions

How should an auto repair shop respond to an overcharging review?

Acknowledge the surprise, explain your approval-before-work policy, and invite the customer to review the invoice with you by phone. Do not post prices or argue line items publicly.

Should I respond to reviews about repeat repairs?

Yes. Mention your warranty and invite them back for a re-check. It shows future customers you stand behind your work.

Is it okay to name the technician in a reply?

On positive reviews where the customer named them, yes. On negative reviews, avoid naming staff publicly.

How quickly should a repair shop reply to reviews?

Within one to two days. Fast replies to negative reviews often lead customers to update their rating after the issue is fixed.
