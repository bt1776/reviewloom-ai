Negative review response examples

Replies you can adapt for the bad reviews small businesses actually get · Updated October 20, 2026

A bad review stings, and the blank reply box makes it worse. Below are nine common negative reviews with a reply for each and a short note on why it works. Change the details so it sounds like you, and never paste one word for word on every review. For the full method, read how to respond to Google reviews. If you think a review is fake, see how to respond to fake reviews.

The pattern behind every good reply

  1. Thank them for taking the time to write.
  2. Name the specific problem they described, and apologize for that experience.
  3. Give one way to reach a real person: a phone number, an email you check, or "ask for the owner."
  4. Stop. Two to four sentences is plenty.

Leave out arguments, private details, and public refund offers. Google’s tips for review replies ask owners to keep replies useful and polite and to avoid promotions.

1. Slow service

Sample review: “Waited 45 minutes for two sandwiches. Staff seemed overwhelmed.” 2 stars.

Thank you for telling us, and I’m sorry you waited 45 minutes for lunch. That’s far longer than it should take. We were short-handed that day, which is our problem to fix, not yours. If you’re willing, please call and ask for me so I can make your next visit right.

Why it works: it repeats the real complaint, gives a brief reason without making excuses, and moves the fix offline.

2. Rude employee

Sample review: “The guy at the front desk was dismissive and rolled his eyes when I asked a question.” 1 star.

I’m sorry you felt brushed off when you asked for help. Everyone who walks in should be treated with patience. I’d like to hear more about what happened so I can look into it. Please email me at the address on our profile with the day and time you came in.

Why it works: it takes the feeling seriously without publicly blaming a staff member before you have checked what happened.

3. Wrong or missing order

Sample review: “Ordered for pickup and got home to find half of it missing.” 2 stars.

That’s frustrating, and I’m sorry you got home to an incomplete order. Please call us with the name on the order and we’ll sort it out with you directly. We’re also adding a second check at the pickup counter so this doesn’t happen again.

Why it works: it offers a concrete fix in private and mentions a real process change. Only mention a change you are actually making.

4. “Too expensive”

Sample review: “Way overpriced for what you get.” 2 stars.

Thanks for the honest feedback. We price our work around licensed technicians and parts that carry a warranty, but we know that only matters if the value is clear when you visit. If you’d like to talk through your invoice, please give me a call. I’m happy to walk you through it.

Why it works: it explains value calmly once, does not discount in public, and offers a conversation.

5. Missed or late appointment

Sample review: “Booked a 9 AM window, nobody showed until after noon and no one called.” 1 star.

I’m sorry we were three hours late and didn’t call to let you know. You planned your morning around us, and we let you down. Please reach out to me directly so I can apologize properly and make sure your next appointment is on time.

Why it works: it owns the missed call, which is usually the part that makes people angriest.

6. Product broke or didn’t work

Sample review: “The blender I bought here stopped working after a week.” 2 stars.

I’m sorry your blender quit after only a week. That shouldn’t happen. Bring it in with your receipt, or call us, and we’ll walk you through the return or warranty options.

Why it works: it points to the policy you already have instead of inventing a new promise in public.

7. 1-star rating with no text

Sample review: no text, 1 star.

We’re sorry your visit fell short. We’d really like to know what happened so we can do better. Please call or email us any time and ask for the owner.

Why it works: it is short, does not guess at the problem, and shows later readers that every rating gets a response.

8. A review that looks fake or isn’t a customer

Sample review: “Worst place ever. Total scam.” 1 star, from an account you can’t match to any visit.

We take every review seriously, but we can’t find a record of your visit. If you were a customer, please call us so we can understand what happened and help.

Why it works: it stays polite, quietly tells readers the visit may not be real, and leaves room for a genuine customer to respond. Flag the review in your Business Profile too. Google explains how to report a review that breaks its policies.

9. Mixed 3-star review

Sample review: “Food was great, but the music was so loud we couldn’t talk.” 3 stars.

Thank you, and I’m glad you enjoyed the food. You’re right about the volume. Several guests have mentioned it, and we’ve turned it down during dinner hours. I hope you’ll come back and enjoy a quieter meal with us.

Why it works: it thanks them for the praise, agrees with the fair point, and says what changed.

Phrases to avoid

  • “We’re sorry you feel that way.” It sounds like you are blaming them for being upset.
  • “That never happens here.” Readers just saw that it did.
  • “Please remove this review.” Deal with the experience first. Never offer anything in exchange for changing a review.
  • Any customer name, order detail, or health information they didn’t already share publicly.

Questions owners ask

How do you respond to a negative review professionally?

Thank the reviewer, apologize for the specific experience they describe without confirming facts you have not checked, give one way to reach you directly, and stop after two or three short sentences. Do not argue or offer compensation in public.

Should I respond to a 1-star review with no text?

Yes. A short reply that thanks them and invites them to share what went wrong shows later readers that you take every rating seriously.

What if a negative review is fake?

Flag it in your Google Business Profile, then post a calm reply that says you cannot find a record of the visit and asks the reviewer to contact you. Do not accuse them of lying.

Can I offer a refund in a review reply?

It is better to invite them to contact you and discuss it privately. Public offers can look like you are paying for a better review, and Google asks businesses not to use replies for promotions or incentives.
