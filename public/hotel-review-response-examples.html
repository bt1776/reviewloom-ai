Hotel review response examples

For hotels, inns, B&Bs, and vacation rentals · Updated October 30, 2026

Travelers read reviews, and your replies, before they book. A thoughtful response to a bad stay can win over the next guest, and a warm reply to a great one reminds people why they should choose you. Below is a simple formula and ten copy-ready replies for the reviews hotels and short-term rentals get most. They work on Google, TripAdvisor, and booking sites. For general tips, read how to respond to Google reviews.

A quick formula for hotel replies

  1. Thank the guest for staying and for the feedback.
  2. Mention a specific detail from their review so it’s clearly not a copied reply.
  3. Apologize for what went wrong and say what you’ve changed.
  4. Move private details, like billing or booking questions, to the front desk or email.
  5. Invite them back, and sign with a real name and title.

Never post a guest’s room number, stay dates, or anything else from your booking records in a public reply.

1. Noisy room or thin walls

Sample review: “Couldn’t sleep. We heard the people next door all night and the ice machine kept clanking.” 2 stars.

I’m sorry you had such a rough night. Noise is the one thing that can ruin a stay no matter how nice the room is. We’ve asked maintenance to check the ice machine on your floor, and our front desk can now offer quieter rooms away from the elevator and ice machines when guests ask at check-in. I hope you’ll let us give you a better night’s sleep next time. Dana, General Manager

Why it works: It accepts the complaint, names a concrete fix, and gives future readers a useful tip.

2. Room wasn’t clean

Sample review: “Hair in the shower and a stain on the sheets. Gross.” 1 star.

This isn’t the standard we hold ourselves to, and I’m truly sorry. I’ve shared your review with our housekeeping lead so we can retrain the team on bathroom and linen checks. If you’re open to it, please reach out to me directly at the front desk so I can make this right. Dana, General Manager

Why it works: Cleanliness complaints scare off future guests, so a clear apology and a specific change matter more than anything else here.

3. Long wait at check-in

Sample review: “Waited 45 minutes to check in after a long drive. Only one person at the desk.” 3 stars.

Thank you for your patience, and I’m sorry your arrival started with a long line after a long drive. We’ve added a second desk shift on our busiest evenings so this doesn’t happen again. I’m glad the rest of your stay went well, and we’d love to welcome you back. Dana, General Manager

Why it works: It doesn’t make excuses about staffing and tells readers what changed.

4. Surprise fees at checkout

Sample review: “Nobody told us about the resort fee and parking charge until checkout.” 2 stars.

I’m sorry the fees caught you off guard. Nobody likes surprises on the bill. Our resort fee covers parking, Wi-Fi, and pool access, and we’ve updated our booking confirmation so it lists every fee up front. If you think a charge was applied in error, please contact our front desk and we’ll review your bill with you. Dana, General Manager

Why it works: It explains the fee once, without arguing, and points the guest to a private channel for billing details.

5. Photos didn’t match the room

Sample review: “Room looked nothing like the pictures online. Dated and smaller than expected.” 2 stars.

Thank you for the honest feedback. You should get the room you expected when you book. We’re updating our listing photos to show each room type as it looks today, and our standard rooms are next on our renovation list. I’m sorry we fell short this time. Dana, General Manager

Why it works: Owning the mismatch builds more trust than defending old photos.

6. Problem with amenities

Sample review: “Pool was closed the whole weekend and no one warned us.” 2 stars.

I’m sorry the pool was closed during your stay, and even more sorry no one told you ahead of time. We should have emailed every guest with a booking that weekend. We now send a notice whenever an amenity is closed for repairs. We hope you’ll come back and enjoy it. Dana, General Manager

Why it works: It separates the closure, which may have been unavoidable, from the real mistake, which was not telling the guest.

7. Rude staff member

Sample review: “The night clerk was rude and acted like we were bothering him.” 1 star.

I’m sorry for how you were treated. Every guest should feel welcome at any hour. I’m following up with our night team personally. If you’re willing to share more details, please contact me directly so I can look into it. Dana, General Manager

Why it works: It never names or blames the employee in public and moves details to a private conversation.

8. Vacation rental check-in confusion

Sample review: “The lockbox code didn’t work and it took an hour to reach the host.” 3 stars.

I’m so sorry you were stuck outside after your trip. That’s the last thing anyone wants. We’ve switched to a keypad lock with a code that’s tested before every arrival, and we now include a backup phone number in the check-in message. Thank you for the kind words about the house, and we’d love to host you again. Sam, Host

Why it works: Small hosts can show the same professionalism as big hotels by naming a fix and a backup plan.

9. Glowing 5-star stay

Sample review: “Spotless room, comfy bed, and Luis at the front desk gave us the best dinner recommendations!” 5 stars.

Thank you so much for staying with us! I’ve passed your note along to Luis, who loves helping guests find great local food. We’re glad you slept well, and we hope to welcome you back on your next trip. Dana, General Manager

Why it works: It names the staff member and the detail the guest loved, which makes it feel personal.

10. Short 5-star review with no text

Sample review: 5 stars, no comment.

Thank you for the 5-star rating! We’re glad you enjoyed your stay and hope to see you again soon. Dana, General Manager

Why it works: A short thank-you is enough. Keep it warm and vary the wording so your replies don’t look copied.

Mistakes hotels make in review replies

  • Pasting the same reply under every review. Guests notice, and it reads as automated.
  • Arguing about whether the room really was dirty or loud. Readers side with the calmer voice.
  • Sharing booking details or blaming a named employee in public.
  • Offering refunds or free nights in exchange for changing a review.
  • Only replying to bad reviews. Thanking happy guests shows you care about everyone.

Questions hotel owners ask

Should a hotel respond to every review?

Aim to reply to every review you can, and always reply to negative ones. Future guests read your replies when they compare places to stay, so a calm, specific answer helps even when the original review is harsh.

How fast should a hotel reply to a review?

Within a day or two is a good target. A quick reply shows guests you pay attention, and it gives an unhappy guest a chance to hear from you before the complaint spreads.

Should I mention the guest's room number or booking details?

No. Never post room numbers, dates of stay, booking details, or anything you know about a guest from your records. Invite them to contact you privately instead.

Can I offer a discount for changing a bad review?

No. Offering anything in exchange for changing or removing a review breaks the rules of most review sites. You can make things right with a guest, but don't tie it to their review.

Who should sign the reply?

A real person, such as the general manager or owner. A name and title make the reply feel personal and show that someone senior is listening.
