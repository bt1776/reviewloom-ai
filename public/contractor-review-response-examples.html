Contractor and home services review response examples

For plumbers, electricians, HVAC, roofers, remodelers, cleaners, and landscapers · Updated October 23, 2026

Homeowners read reviews closely before letting someone into their home, and one bad reply can cost you a big job. Below are nine common contractor and home services reviews with a reply for each and why it works. Swap in your own names, trades, and details. For the general method, read how to respond to Google reviews.

A quick formula for home services business replies

  1. Thank them and mention the job (a water heater install, a roof repair, a kitchen remodel).
  2. If something went wrong, apologize once and say what you changed, such as scheduling, communication, or cleanup.
  3. Move pricing disputes, warranty claims, and callbacks to a phone call.
  4. Sign as the owner, like “Dana, Owner, Tluczek Plumbing,” so homeowners know who stands behind the work.

1. No-show or missed appointment

Sample review: “Took a day off work and the tech never showed. No call, nothing.” 1 star.

I’m sorry. You took a day off and we didn’t show or call, and that’s on us. We’ve since added automatic text alerts when a tech is running late or a job has to move. Please call me at (555) 123-4567; I’d like to get you on the schedule at a time that works and make this right. – Dana, Owner

Why it works: It owns the miss completely, names a process fix, and offers a direct path to the owner.

2. Project ran weeks over schedule

Sample review: “Bathroom remodel was supposed to take 3 weeks and took 7.” 2 stars.

Thank you for your patience through a project that went far longer than we promised. Some delays came from back-ordered tile, but we should have communicated each change sooner and more clearly. We now send weekly schedule updates on every remodel. I’d welcome a call at (555) 123-4567 to talk it through. – Dana, Owner

Why it works: It gives one honest reason without hiding behind it, and shows what is different now.

3. Final bill higher than the estimate

Sample review: “Quote was $4,500, final invoice was $6,200. Felt blindsided.” 2 stars.

I’m sorry the final cost was a surprise. Our policy is to get written approval before any work beyond the original estimate, and if that didn’t happen clearly, we need to fix it. Please call me directly at (555) 123-4567 so we can go through the invoice together. – Dana, Owner

Why it works: It states the policy, avoids debating line items in public, and invites a private review of the bill.

4. Left a mess

Sample review: “The work looks fine but they left drywall dust everywhere and screws in my driveway.” 3 stars.

Thank you for the feedback, and I’m glad the work turned out well. Leaving a mess isn’t acceptable, especially screws in a driveway. We’ve reinforced our cleanup checklist with every crew. If you’d like, we’ll send someone back to finish the cleanup. Just call (555) 123-4567. – Dana

Why it works: It acknowledges the positive, takes the safety issue seriously, and offers a concrete make-good privately.

5. Repair didn’t last

Sample review: “They fixed our AC in June and it broke again in August.” 2 stars.

We’re sorry your AC failed again in the middle of summer. Our repairs come with a warranty, and we want to take a look as soon as possible. Please call our service line at (555) 123-4567 and mention this review so we can prioritize you.

Why it works: It points to the warranty and offers fast, specific help without arguing about the cause.

6. Price complaint

Sample review: “Way overpriced. Got three quotes and these guys were the highest.” 3 stars.

Thank you for considering us and for the honest feedback. Our prices reflect licensed, insured technicians and a 2-year labor warranty, but we understand everyone has a budget. We’re always happy to walk through a quote line by line if that helps.

Why it works: It explains value without being defensive and leaves the door open.

7. Glowing 5-star review

Sample review: “Mike showed up on time, explained everything, and fixed our leak in an hour. Super clean and professional.” 5 stars.

Thank you! We’ll make sure Mike sees this; on time, clear, and clean is exactly the standard we aim for. We’re glad your leak is fixed, and we’re here whenever you need us. – Dana, Owner

Why it works: It repeats the specific praise and passes it to the tech by name, since the reviewer used it.

8. Emergency call praise

Sample review: “Pipe burst at 11pm on a Sunday and they had someone here in 40 minutes. Lifesavers.” 5 stars.

Thank you for trusting us with a late-night emergency. A burst pipe is stressful, and we’re glad we could get there fast. Hope everything is dry now!

Why it works: It recognizes the stress and keeps it short and human.

9. Review that may be a competitor or wrong company

Sample review: “Terrible work, never using them again.” (No record of this customer.) 1 star.

We take every review seriously, but we can’t find a record of this job under your name. We’d like to understand what happened. Please call us at (555) 123-4567 so we can look into it.

Why it works: It stays polite, signals to readers that the review may be mistaken, and invites contact. You can also flag the review with Google.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Blaming the homeowner, the supplier, or a subcontractor in public.
  • Posting invoice amounts, addresses, or job details the customer didn’t share.
  • Getting into a back-and-forth about workmanship on Google instead of offering a site visit.
  • Ignoring 5-star reviews, which are your best free marketing.

Frequently asked questions

Should contractors respond to every review?

Yes, if you can. Homeowners compare contractors closely, and replies show you are responsive and stand behind your work. Prioritize negative reviews first.

How do I respond to a review disputing a bill?

Acknowledge the frustration, state your general policy on estimates and change orders, and invite the customer to call so you can go through the invoice privately. Do not post amounts or argue line items.

What if the review is from someone who was never a customer?

Reply politely that you cannot find a record of the job and invite them to contact you, then flag the review in Google Business Profile as not relevant or conflict of interest.

Can I offer a discount in my reply?

Avoid public discounts. Offer a site visit or a callback privately instead, which fixes the problem without inviting others to complain for a deal.
