How to respond to a review that says you're too expensive

A practical guide for small-business owners · Updated November 11, 2026

"Overpriced" is one of the most common complaints in small-business reviews, and one of the easiest to get wrong. The urge is to defend your prices or knock something off to make it go away. Neither helps. People reading your profile later want to know two things: whether you're worth it, and whether you'll be straight with them about cost. A good reply answers both. For general advice, start with how to respond to Google reviews.

The short version

  • Thank them for the feedback, even if you disagree.
  • Don't argue about the number. You won't win, and readers will side with the customer.
  • Explain your value in one sentence: what's included, what you use, or what you guarantee.
  • Own any surprise. If the bill was higher than they expected, that's the real problem.
  • Never discount in public. Offer to talk directly instead.

First, figure out what they're really saying

Price complaints usually fall into one of three types. "It cost more than I expected" is about communication, so fix the surprise. "It wasn't worth it" is about quality or experience, so address what fell short. "You're more expensive than the place down the street" is about value, so explain what's different without naming anyone. Your reply should match the type, not just the word "expensive."

1. The bill was higher than the estimate

Thank you for the feedback, Chris. You're right that we should have called you before doing the extra brake work, and I'm sorry the final bill caught you off guard. We've made it a rule that every change to an estimate gets your approval first. I'd like to walk through the invoice with you. Please call me at (555) 123-4567.

Why it works: it owns the actual failure, the surprise, instead of defending the price of the parts.

2. "Way too pricey for what you get"

Thanks for taking the time to share this, Dana. We're sorry the meal didn't feel worth the price. We make everything from scratch with local ingredients, so we know our prices are higher than some, and that means every plate has to deliver. We'd love to hear what fell short. Please email me at owner@example.com.

Why it works: one sentence of value, no lecture, and an honest admission that the experience has to match the price.

3. "Cheaper elsewhere"

Thank you for the review, Sam. We understand there are less expensive options, and we appreciate you giving us a try. Every cleaning includes a full exam and X-rays when needed, with no add-on fees later. If you'd like a clear breakdown of what's covered, our office manager is happy to go over it at (555) 987-6543.

Why it works: it doesn't name or criticize competitors, and it makes the value concrete. More examples for clinics are on our dentist review replies page.

4. A 5-star review that mentions price

Thank you so much, Alex! We know a full color service is an investment, and we're thrilled you felt it was worth it. See you in eight weeks!

Why it works: when a happy customer brings up price, a quick nod reinforces the value for everyone reading. See more positive review response examples.

5. A low rating that only says "overpriced"

Thanks for your feedback, Jamie. We're sorry the visit didn't feel like good value. We'd genuinely like to understand what you expected, so please reach out at hello@example.com whenever you have a minute.

Why it works: short, not defensive, and it invites detail privately instead of guessing.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Listing your costs, rent, or wages to justify the price.
  • Naming competitors or comparing prices with them.
  • Offering a discount, refund, or coupon in the public reply.
  • Implying the customer can't afford you ("we're not for everyone").
  • Sharing their invoice details or account information.
  • Pasting the same "quality costs more" line under every price complaint.

What to do after you reply

If several reviews mention surprise charges, the fix is in your process, not your replies: clearer estimates, approval before extra work, or prices posted where customers can see them. Questions owners ask

Should I respond to reviews that say my prices are too high?

Yes. Future customers read these replies to decide whether you're worth the price. A short, calm reply that explains what's included and offers to talk does more for you than silence or an argument.

Should I lower my price or offer a discount in the reply?

No, not in public. A public discount teaches readers that complaining earns money off and can upset customers who paid full price. If a credit is fair, handle it privately.

What if the real problem was a surprise charge?

Then own that, not the price. Say you should have explained or approved the cost up front, say what you're changing, and invite them to go over the bill with you directly.

Should I mention competitors' prices?

No. Naming competitors or comparing prices in a reply reads as defensive. Talk about what your customers get from you instead.
