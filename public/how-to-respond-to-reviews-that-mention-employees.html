How to respond to reviews that mention an employee by name

A practical guide for small-business owners · Updated November 3, 2026

Many of the best reviews a small business gets are about a person: "Maria cut my hair perfectly," "Jake explained the repair and didn't upsell me." Some of the hardest ones are too: "The guy at the front desk was rude." Replying well to both kinds builds trust with future customers and protects your team. This guide covers when to use the employee's name, how to handle complaints about a staff member, and examples you can adapt. For general advice, start with how to respond to Google reviews.

The short version

  • Praise: thank the reviewer, name the employee (with their OK), and say you passed the compliment on.
  • Complaint: apologize for the experience, don't repeat the employee's name, don't blame or defend them in public, and move the conversation offline.
  • Never discuss discipline, training problems, or anyone's personal details in a public reply.

Before you reply: check with your team

Ask staff once whether they're comfortable being named in public replies. Most people love it, but some prefer privacy, and first names only is a good default. If a review uses a full name or a nickname, you can still reply with just the first name.

1. A 5-star review that praises an employee

Thank you, Lauren! We'll make sure Maria sees this. She takes real pride in getting every cut just right, and it means a lot to her to hear it. We'll see you at your next appointment!

Why it works: it names the employee, says the praise will be passed on, and adds a human detail without overdoing it. Then actually share the review with Maria.

Thanks for the kind words about Jake, Chris. Clear explanations and honest recommendations are exactly what we want every customer at Valley Auto Care to get. We appreciate you trusting us with your car.

Why it works: it connects the employee's behavior to what the business stands for, which reads well to future customers comparing shops.

2. A review that praises several people

What a great note, Tom. Thank you! Ana, Luis, and the whole install crew will be thrilled to read this. We're glad the new patio came out exactly how you pictured it.

Why it works: it credits everyone named without turning into a list of thank-yous.

3. A mixed review: great employee, other problems

Thank you for the honest feedback, Dana. We're glad Priya took such good care of you, and we'll share your kind words with her. We're sorry about the long wait to be seated. We're adjusting our weekend staffing, and we'd love another chance to get the whole visit right.

Why it works: it gives the employee credit and owns the problem as a business issue, not anyone's personal failure.

4. A negative review that criticizes an employee

Hi Mark, thank you for letting us know, and we're sorry your visit felt unwelcoming. That isn't the experience we want anyone to have. Our manager, Kelly, would like to hear more and make it right. Please reach her at (555) 123-4567 or kelly@yourbusiness.com.

Why it works: it doesn't repeat the staff member's name next to the complaint, doesn't argue with the reviewer, and doesn't throw the employee under the bus. It gives a real person to contact so the issue gets handled privately.

What to avoid: "We've spoken to Jason and he's been written up" or "Jason is one of our best employees, so we doubt that happened." The first exposes a personnel matter; the second calls the customer a liar. Both look bad to everyone reading.

5. A complaint that names someone who doesn't work for you

Thank you for the review. We don't have a team member by that name and couldn't find a matching visit, so we'd like to learn more. Please contact us at (555) 123-4567 so we can look into what happened.

Why it works: it stays polite and factual. The review may be meant for another business, or it may be fake. If it breaks Google's policies, you can report it. See how to respond to fake reviews.

6. A review about an employee who has since left

Thank you for the wonderful review, Sarah! Ben has moved on to a new chapter, but we've passed your kind words along, and our team is carrying on the same care. We'd love to see you again.

Why it works: it's gracious, doesn't share details about why the person left, and reassures the customer that the service they liked continues.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Naming an employee in a reply to a complaint, even to defend them.
  • Mentioning discipline, firing, or training problems in public.
  • Sharing an employee's last name, schedule, or other personal details.
  • Using the same "We'll pass this on!" line under every review. Add one specific detail each time.
  • Letting the praise stop at the reply. Tell the employee; it's one of the cheapest morale boosts you have.

Turn reviews into team recognition

Reviews that name staff are useful beyond the reply. Many owners read them out in a team meeting, post them on a break-room board, or tie them to a small recognition. Customers notice when the people they praised are still smiling the next time they visit, and the employees learn what customers value most.

Questions owners ask

Should I use the employee's name in my reply?

For praise, yes, if the employee is comfortable with it; using their first name shows you noticed and makes the reply personal. For a complaint, usually no. Don't repeat a staff member's name next to criticism, and speak for the business instead.

How do I respond when a review criticizes a specific employee?

Thank the reviewer, apologize for the experience without blaming or defending the person, say you are looking into it, and give a direct way to reach a manager so you can sort it out privately.

Should I tell the employee about a positive review?

Yes. Share it with them and the team. Recognizing praise from real customers is good for morale and encourages more of the service people are writing about.

Can I discipline or discuss an employee's performance in a review reply?

No. Never mention discipline, training problems, or personnel details in public. Handle that privately and keep the public reply about the customer's experience.
