How to respond to fake Google reviews

What to do when a review isn’t from a real customer · Updated October 22, 2026

Few things make a small-business owner angrier than a 1-star review from someone who never walked in the door. The good news is that you have two tools: reporting the review to Google, and a calm public reply that shows future customers how you handle problems. This guide covers both, with reply examples you can adapt. For ordinary complaints, see these negative review response examples, and for the full method, read how to respond to Google reviews.

First, make sure it’s really fake

Before you call a review fake, check your records. Search your bookings, invoices, or point-of-sale system for the name and the date. Ask the staff who were working. Many reviews that look fake are real customers using a nickname, a family member’s account, or a different email than the one on file. If the review is real, treat it as a normal complaint.

Signs a review may be fake include a reviewer with no history or dozens of 1-star reviews on unrelated businesses, details that don’t match anything you sell, a burst of similar reviews in a short time, or a review that clearly describes a different business.

Step 1: Report it to Google

From your Google Business Profile you can report a review that breaks Google’s rules, and later check the status of your report. Google explains the process in Report inappropriate reviews on your Business Profile. Reviews that may qualify include spam and fake engagement, conflicts of interest such as a competitor or former employee posting, off-topic content, and harassment, as listed in Google’s prohibited and restricted content policy. Google makes the final call, and a review is not removed just because it is negative or unfair.

Step 2: Post a calm public reply

Removal can take a while, and it may not happen. In the meantime, potential customers are reading the review. A short, polite reply tells them you checked and you care. Follow this pattern:

  1. Thank them for the feedback, even though it stings.
  2. Say plainly that you couldn’t find a record of their visit, without calling them a liar.
  3. Invite them to contact you directly, with a real phone number or email.
  4. Keep it to two or three sentences, and don’t argue in the comments.

1. Reviewer who was never a customer

Sample review: “Terrible service, they overcharged me and were rude.” 1 star, and you have no record of this person.

Hi, thank you for your feedback. We take every review seriously, but we couldn’t find a record of a visit or invoice under this name. Please call us at (555) 123-4567 so we can look into what happened and make it right.

Why it works: readers see an owner who checked the facts and offered to help, and the reviewer has no fight to pick.

2. Review meant for a different business

Sample review: “My oil change took three hours.” 1 star, for a bakery.

Hi, we’re sorry about your experience, but it sounds like this review may be for a different business. We’re Main Street Bakery in Surprise, AZ, and we don’t offer auto services. If we’ve got that wrong, please reach out at hello@example.com and we’ll be glad to help.

Why it works: it’s friendly, it clears things up for anyone reading, and it leaves the door open in case you’re mistaken.

3. Suspected competitor or former employee

Sample review: “Avoid this place, the owner cuts corners on everything.” 1 star, from an account you think is connected to a competitor.

Thank you for sharing this. We stand behind our work and haven’t been able to match this review to any job we’ve done. If you were a customer, please contact us directly at (555) 123-4567 so we can talk it through.

Why it works: it never names or accuses anyone. Report the conflict of interest to Google and let the reply speak for your business.

4. Star rating with no text from an unknown reviewer

Sample review: no text, 1 star.

We’re sorry to see this rating and couldn’t find a record of your visit. We’d love to hear what happened, so please reach out at hello@example.com.

Why it works: short and neutral. There’s nothing to rebut, so don’t invent a story.

5. One of a sudden wave of bad reviews

Sample review: one of several 1-star reviews posted within a day, with similar wording and no matching customers.

Thank you for your review. We’ve noticed several reviews today that we can’t connect to any customer visit, and we’ve reported them to Google. If you did visit us, please call (555) 123-4567 and we’ll gladly look into it.

Why it works: it tells readers something unusual is going on without sounding defensive. Report each review separately.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Calling the reviewer a liar, a scammer, or a competitor by name. If you’re wrong, it’s a real customer you just insulted in public.
  • Posting long, angry rebuttals. Readers skim, and the longest reply in the thread usually looks worst.
  • Sharing private details about a real customer to prove a point.
  • Asking friends or staff to post reviews to bury the fake one. That also breaks Google’s rules and can put your own reviews at risk.

Questions owners ask

Should I respond to a fake Google review?

Usually yes. Report it to Google first, then post a short, calm reply so future customers see that you checked your records and invited the reviewer to contact you. Removal can take time or may not happen, and a calm reply protects you either way.

Can I get a fake Google review removed?

You can report a review that breaks Google’s content policy, such as spam, fake engagement, conflicts of interest, or off-topic content, from your Business Profile. Google decides whether to remove it, and a review is not removed just because it is negative.

Should I accuse the reviewer of lying?

No. Say you could not find a record of their visit and invite them to contact you. Readers trust a calm owner more than an angry one, and an accusation can backfire if the review turns out to be real.

What if the review is meant for a different business?

Reply politely that it looks like the review may be for another business, give your name and location so readers can tell, and report it to Google as off-topic.
