How to respond to old reviews

A practical guide for small-business owners · Updated November 5, 2026

Plenty of owners open their Google Business Profile and find months, or years, of reviews nobody answered. It's tempting to leave them alone because replying now feels awkward. In most cases a late reply is still better than none: future customers read your old reviews too, and an unanswered complaint near the top of your profile keeps costing you. This guide covers when to reply, what order to work in, and examples you can adapt. For the basics, start with how to respond to Google reviews.

The short version

  • Reply anyway. A late, sincere reply beats silence.
  • Acknowledge the delay in a few words, without excuses.
  • Keep it short and specific to what they wrote.
  • Mention what's changed if the complaint is about something you've since fixed.
  • Work newest-negative first, then the rest.

Is it too late to reply?

Rarely. Shoppers sort by "newest" and "most relevant," so older reviews keep showing up, and the replies under them tell people how you treat customers today. Google also generally notifies the reviewer when you respond, so assume they may read it. The main exception is a review so old that the business has changed completely (new owner, new location, new menu). Even then, a short note that things have changed is useful to readers.

What order to work in

  1. Negative reviews from the last few months.
  2. Older negative reviews that still appear near the top of your profile.
  3. Recent positive reviews, especially ones that mention a person or service by name.
  4. Older positive reviews with text.
  5. Star-only reviews with no text, which can get a one-line thanks or be skipped. See reviews with no text.

Ten or fifteen replies a day is a realistic pace. Spreading them out keeps the replies thoughtful instead of copy-pasted.

1. Old negative review about a problem you've fixed

Hi Maria, I'm sorry for the very slow reply, and sorry your appointment ran so late. You were right that our scheduling wasn't working. Since then we've moved to online booking with reminder texts, and wait times are much shorter. If you're ever willing to give us another try, ask for me and I'll make sure it goes smoothly.

Why it works: one phrase owns the delay, the reply agrees with the specific complaint, and it tells future readers the problem is fixed.

2. Old negative review you can't do much about now

Daniel, thank you for taking the time to write this, and I apologize that it took us so long to respond. Leaving without a clear quote isn't the experience we want anyone to have. If you'd still like to talk it through, you can reach me directly at (555) 123-4567.

Why it works: it doesn't pretend the moment hasn't passed, and it still offers a real way to reach someone.

3. Old positive review

Thanks so much, Kevin, and sorry we're only now saying so! We're glad the deck has held up to the Arizona summer. We appreciate you recommending us to your neighbors.

Why it works: it's warm, short, and mentions the specific job, which also helps your profile show up for that service.

4. Review from before a change in ownership

Thank you for the feedback, Lisa. We took over the shop last spring and have made a lot of changes, including new stylists and longer weekend hours. We'd love the chance to show you the difference.

Why it works: it explains the change factually without blaming the previous owner, and it gives the reader a reason to look again.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Pasting the same reply under fifty old reviews in one afternoon. Readers notice, and it looks automated.
  • Long explanations of why you didn't reply sooner. One phrase is enough.
  • Arguing with an old complaint. If the facts are wrong, correct them politely for future readers.
  • Offering discounts in public to old reviewers. Make it right privately if you want to.
  • Asking anyone to update or remove their rating.

Stay caught up afterward

Once the backlog is cleared, set a simple habit: check new reviews two or three times a week and reply within a day or two. Recent replies matter most, and a short, specific reply now is easier than a long catch-up later. Questions owners ask

Is it too late to respond to an old Google review?

Usually not. Future customers still read old reviews, and a late reply shows the business is paying attention now. Acknowledge the delay in a few words and keep the reply short.

Does the reviewer get notified when I reply to an old review?

Google generally notifies reviewers when the business replies, so assume the person may see it. Write as if they will, and avoid anything you wouldn't want them to read today.

Which old reviews should I reply to first?

Start with the newest negative reviews, then older negative ones that are still near the top of your profile, then recent positive reviews. Very old 5-star reviews with no text can wait or be skipped.

Should I apologize for replying late?

Briefly, yes. One short phrase such as 'Sorry for the slow reply' is enough. Don't make excuses or explain at length why you missed it.
