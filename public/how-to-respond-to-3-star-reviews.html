How to respond to a 3-star review

A practical guide for small-business owners · Updated November 4, 2026

A 3-star review is the most winnable review you'll get. The customer liked something enough to say so, but something else let them down. Unlike a furious 1-star, they're usually open to coming back, and the people reading your profile pay close attention to how you handle the middle of the road. This guide covers what to say, what to skip, and examples you can adapt. For general advice, start with how to respond to Google reviews.

The short version

  • Thank them by name for taking the time to write.
  • Credit the good part they mentioned, specifically.
  • Own the miss in one plain sentence, with no excuses and no "but."
  • Say what changes, if something is actually changing.
  • Invite them back or give a direct way to reach you.

Why 3-star reviews deserve your best reply

Shoppers skim the 5-star reviews and often read the middle ones closely, because that's where the honest detail is. A calm, specific reply under a mixed review shows you take feedback seriously, which can matter more to a future customer than another glowing rating. It's also your best chance to turn a lukewarm customer into a regular.

1. Good product, slow service

Thanks for the honest review, Jenna. We're glad you loved the brisket tacos! You're right that the wait on Saturday was too long, and that's on us. We've added another cook for weekend evenings, and we'd love to have you back to show you the faster version of us.

Why it works: it names what they liked, admits the wait without blaming the crowd, and gives a concrete fix.

2. Good work, poor communication

Thank you, Robert. We're happy the new water heater is working well. We should have called when the part was delayed instead of leaving you guessing, and we're sorry about that. We now text every customer when a schedule changes. If you need anything else, call me directly at (555) 123-4567.

Why it works: it separates the quality of the job from the communication problem and shows a process change, which reassures future customers who worry about the same thing.

3. "It was fine" with no detail

Thanks for stopping in, Alex. We want every visit to be better than just fine, so if there's anything we could have done to make it great, we'd really like to hear it. Reach us anytime at hello@yourbusiness.com.

Why it works: it stays upbeat, doesn't sound wounded, and asks for the detail you need to improve. Keep it short; there isn't much to respond to.

4. Price complaint

Thank you for the feedback, Priya, and we're glad the color came out the way you wanted. We understand the price was more than you expected. We'll make sure our team walks through costs before the appointment starts so there are no surprises next time.

Why it works: it doesn't argue about whether the price was fair or explain your margins. It addresses the real problem, which was the surprise, not the number.

5. A mixed review with a factual error

Thanks for the review, Sam. We're glad the staff made your dog feel at ease. One quick note for anyone reading: our Sunday hours are 10 to 4, so we're sorry if an old listing sent you here early. We've updated our profile, and we hope to see you both again soon.

Why it works: it corrects the record politely, for future readers rather than to win the argument, and still thanks the reviewer.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Starting the apology with "but" or "unfortunately" right after the thanks. It reads like an excuse.
  • Explaining why the problem wasn't really your fault (short-staffed, busy night, supplier issue). Customers don't care whose fault it was.
  • Asking them to raise their rating.
  • Pasting the same generic "Sorry you weren't satisfied" line under every mixed review.
  • Offering a refund or discount in public. If you want to make it right, do it privately.

What to do after you reply

Mixed reviews are free consulting. If several mention the same weak spot, such as wait times, follow-up calls, or pricing surprises, that's the fix most likely to raise your average. Keep a simple list of what each 3-star review mentions and look at it once a month. For replies to angrier reviews, see negative review response examples.

Questions owners ask

Should I respond to 3-star reviews?

Yes. A 3-star review is the reviewer telling you they were close to happy. A thoughtful reply shows future customers you listen, and it is often enough to win the reviewer back for another visit.

What should a reply to a mixed review include?

Thank the reviewer by name, mention the specific thing they liked, acknowledge the specific thing that fell short without excuses, say what you're doing about it if you can, and invite them back or give a way to reach you.

Should I ask the reviewer to change their rating?

No. Don't ask for a higher rating in your reply. Fix the problem and invite them back; some people update their review on their own after a good second visit.

How fast should I reply to a 3-star review?

Within a day or two if you can. Mixed reviews are the easiest to turn around, and a quick reply shows the feedback reached someone who cares.
