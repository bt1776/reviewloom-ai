How to respond to a negative review without admitting fault

A practical guide for small-business owners · Updated November 13, 2026

A harsh review lands, and you're stuck between two bad options: apologize and risk sounding like you're accepting blame, or defend yourself and look like you're picking a fight. There's a middle path. You can show real empathy, stay professional, and still avoid admitting to something you haven't confirmed. For the basics, start with how to respond to Google reviews.

This is general communication advice, not legal advice. If a review involves an injury, property damage, or a legal threat, talk to your insurer or attorney before replying in detail.

The short version

  • Apologize for the experience, not the blame. "I'm sorry your visit was frustrating" rather than "I'm sorry we messed up."
  • Thank them for the feedback. It shows you're listening without conceding anything.
  • Stick to neutral facts, like your policy, and skip the play-by-play.
  • Never share private details about the customer or their visit.
  • Move it offline with a name and a phone number or email.

Empathy vs. admission: the key difference

Empathy describes how the customer feels. An admission describes what your business did. "I understand how disappointing that must have been" is empathy. "Our technician damaged your car" is an admission. You can be generous with the first while you look into the facts. If you check your records and you were clearly in the wrong, it's usually better to own it plainly (see 1-star review replies), but don't concede in public before you know.

Phrases that work

  • "I'm sorry to hear your experience didn't meet your expectations."
  • "Thank you for taking the time to share this. We take feedback like this seriously."
  • "I'd like to learn more about what happened."
  • "This isn't the experience we want anyone to have."
  • "Please contact me directly at (555) 123-4567 so I can look into it."

Phrases to avoid

  • "It was our fault" or "we made a mistake" (before you've confirmed it)
  • "Our employee messed up," or naming and blaming staff (see reviews that mention employees)
  • "We'll pay for the damage" or any promise of compensation in public
  • "That's not what happened," "you're lying," or any argument
  • Any private detail: visit dates, what they bought or paid, account or health information

Example replies

A service complaint you haven't verified yet

Hi Dana, thank you for sharing this, and I'm sorry your visit was so frustrating. That's not the experience we want anyone to have. I'd like to understand what happened and see what we can do. Please call me directly at (555) 123-4567. — Alex, Owner

Why it works: warm and quick, apologizes for the frustration, and doesn't confirm or deny the details.

A dispute where the customer has the facts wrong

Thanks for your feedback, Marcus. We do require 24 hours' notice for cancellations, which is posted at booking and on our website, and I understand that can be frustrating when plans change. I'd be glad to talk it through. Please reach out at hello@example.com.

Why it works: one neutral fact about the policy, no "you're wrong," and an offer to talk. For pricing disputes, see how to respond to reviews about price.

A review that mentions damage or an injury

Hi Jordan, thank you for letting us know. We take concerns like this very seriously and want to look into it right away. Please contact me directly at (555) 123-4567 so we can get the details. — Sam, General Manager

Why it works: short, serious, and private. It doesn't describe the incident, assign fault, or promise anything.

A review you suspect is fake

Thank you for the feedback. We take every review seriously, but we can't find a record of this visit. If you were a customer, please contact us at (555) 123-4567 so we can look into it.

Why it works: calm and factual, with no accusation. Report it to Google too: see how to respond to fake reviews.

Industries where this matters most

Dentists and medical offices must be especially careful not to confirm someone was a patient or mention treatment details. Auto shops and contractors often face damage claims. Restaurants get food-safety complaints. For tailored examples, see our dentist, auto repair, contractor, and restaurant pages.

A quick checklist before you post

  • Did I thank them and acknowledge how they feel?
  • Did I avoid saying we caused something I haven't confirmed?
  • Is there any private customer detail in the reply? Remove it.
  • Did I avoid blaming an employee or the customer?
  • Did I give a real name and a way to reach us offline?
  • Would I be comfortable with a future customer, or a lawyer, reading this?

Questions owners ask

Can I apologize in a review reply without admitting fault?

Yes. Apologize for the customer's experience or frustration, such as "I'm sorry your visit was frustrating," rather than for a specific mistake you haven't confirmed. That shows empathy without stating that your business caused the problem.

Should I argue with a negative review that is wrong?

No. Arguing in public makes you look defensive to every future customer. State one neutral fact if it matters, such as your actual policy, then invite the reviewer to contact you directly.

What should I never put in a review reply?

Never share private details about the customer, such as appointment dates, account information, health or medical details, or what they paid. Don't blame named employees, and don't speculate about who was at fault.

What if a review mentions an injury, damage, or legal threat?

Keep the public reply very short and neutral, say you take it seriously and want to look into it, and move the conversation to a phone call or email. Talk to your insurer or attorney before saying anything more specific.
